Scalar perturbations and frame choice in higher-curvature inflation: gauge-invariant variables in the Einstein and Jordan frames

Submitted 9 Apr 2026 UTC (this version) · , Announced 10 Apr 2026, 00:00 UTC · , v1, this version v2, version 2

You are viewing version 1. A newer version is available — version 2, revised 28 Jun 2026.

Lucía Ferreyra, Jonas Brekke, Amara Osei, Kenji Morikawa, Priyanka Deshmukh, Mateus Albuquerque, Ingrid Solheim, Tariq Haddad, Yuna Seo, Olumide Adebayo, Clara Weissmann, Rafael Quintero, Anika Holmberg, Dmitri Volkonsky, Mei-Ling Chou, Samuel Okonkwo, Elif Tanrıverdi, Bastien Carrigou, Nadia Rahimi, Tomasz Wrzosek, Siobhán Gallagher, Arjun Venkataraman

Abstract

We study scalar perturbations during inflation in gravity with quadratic curvature terms, and ask how much of their behavior depends on the choice of conformal frame. Working first in the Einstein frame, we write the equations of motion in terms of three sets of gauge-invariant variables, so that physical effects can be separated from artifacts of the coordinates. On superhorizon scales we find the leading-order behavior of each variable and follow it through several common gauges. One gauge shows an apparent growth of the metric perturbations; we show that the growth disappears in the other gauges and in every observable we compute, so it is not a sign of instability. We then repeat the analysis in the Jordan frame, show which variables carry over unchanged, and explain why results in the two frames agree once they are expressed in gauge-invariant form. The method applies to any higher-curvature model with a scalar degree of freedom.
